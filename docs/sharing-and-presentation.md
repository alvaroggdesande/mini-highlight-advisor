# Sharing & Presentation Playbook

How to put **Mini Highlight Advisor** in front of painters, communities, and paint
companies — in a way that makes them *want* to try it.

Tone target: **polished product**. Rollout: **low-key → feedback → wider reach →
maybe money**. The whole strategy hinges on one thing: this is a *visual* tool, so
**the demo has to be visual and instant.** Nobody reads a paragraph about a paint
overlay. They react to seeing one.

---

## 1. The hook (say this, everywhere, first)

> **Upload a photo of your primed mini → get a paint-by-layer highlight plan drawn
> directly on your own photo.**

The differentiator that makes people stop scrolling:

> "It's not a text guide and it's not an AI hallucination of a mini you don't own.
> It reads the actual light on *your* sculpt and shows you where every highlight
> band goes — on your photo, layer by layer."

That single sentence is your positioning against everything else out there
(PaintGuide.ai = text only, AI-MiniPainter = fake renders, MyMiniScore = scoring).
Nobody else does geometry-driven overlays on the user's own photo. **Lead with that.**

---

## 2. The hero asset: the before/after slider

Everything below depends on this existing. Build it first.

**The single most valuable asset you can make:** a before → after comparison of the
*same* mini photo — raw primed photo on the left, painted highlight preview on the
right. A drag-to-reveal slider is the gold standard (people play with it, and playing
= engagement = shares).

Runner-up assets, in priority order:
1. **A 10–15s screen recording / GIF** of the full flow: upload → lasso a region →
   see the plan appear. Loops silently in a Reddit/Discord feed and does all the
   selling. Keep it under 15s and cut all dead time.
2. **The paint-along step strip** — the dark→light sequence of "where to paint /
   apply across / ends up here" images side by side. This is genuinely novel and
   very screenshottable.
3. **A real result** — one of *your own* minis you actually painted using the plan,
   next to the plan. Proof it works in the real world, not just on screen.

> Capture checklist (you said you'd make these): raking-side-light photo of a primed
> mini (per your own shooting guide), run it through the tool, screenshot each stage
> at high resolution. Do it once, well, and reuse the assets everywhere.

## 3. Staged rollout (your "low-key → money" path)

### Stage 0 — Friends & your painting circle (this week)
- Share the live Render link + the before/after image with 5–10 painting friends.
- Ask **one** specific question, not "what do you think?": *"Would you use this before
  your next highlight session — and if not, what stopped you?"*
- Goal: catch the embarrassing bugs and the "I didn't understand what to do" moments
  before strangers see them. Fix the top 2–3 friction points.

### Stage 1 — Hobbyist communities (once Stage 0 feedback is in)
- Post the GIF to **r/minipainting**, **r/Warhammer40k** painting threads, and 2–3
  active painting Discords you're already in.
- Frame it as *"I built a free tool, would love feedback"* even with a polished
  presentation — communities reward the humble-but-polished combo and punish
  pure-marketing energy.
- Watch for the recurring request. Whatever 5 people ask for is your roadmap.

### Stage 2 — Wider / where it's useful (after community traction)
- Painting YouTubers & content creators: offer it free, let them feature it. One
  creator demoing it is worth 1,000 cold posts. Send the GIF + link, not a wall of text.
- Hobby newsletters, painting blogs, Facebook painting groups.
- A simple landing page (even just the before/after slider + "Try it" button) that
  you can link everywhere instead of the raw app URL.

### Stage 3 — Companies & maybe money (once you have usage + testimonials)
- Now you have proof: "X painters used it, here's what they said." *That's* what makes
  a paint company reply. See §5 for outreach.
- Monetization options to keep in your back pocket (§6) — don't lead with these.

## 4. Channel playbooks

### Reddit (r/minipainting, r/Warhammer40k, r/Warhammer)
- **Post the GIF/video as the post itself** (image/video posts beat link posts).
- Title formula: *"I built a free tool that shows you where to highlight — on your
  own mini's photo"*.
- First comment (from you): the link + one line on what's free and a genuine ask for
  feedback. Reply to *everyone* in the first few hours — early engagement drives reach.
- Read each sub's self-promo rules first; some require a flair or a set day.

### Discord painting servers
- Drop the GIF in the relevant channel (#tools, #resources, #wip) with one sentence.
- Discords are where your **feedback loop** lives — people will tell you exactly what
  breaks. Offer to hop in a voice/thread to hear what they'd want.

### Company outreach (Vallejo, Citadel/GW, AK Interactive)
- Why they'd care: your tool **recommends specific paints per layer from a palette**,
  and you already have all three brands in your catalog. That's a natural fit — a tool
  that tells people which of *their* paints to buy for each highlight band.
- Realistic first ask is **not** money. It's: "Can I feature your paint range in the
  palette?" / "Would you share this with your community?" A brand reshare or an
  official palette blessing is the foot in the door; sponsorship talk comes later.
- Contact the **community / social / marketing** person, not general sales. Find them
  via the brand's Instagram or LinkedIn.

## 6. Friction killers (do these before Stage 1)

A polished product dies on friction. Before public posts, make sure:
- **No login wall.** "Try it" must go straight to uploading a photo.
- **A demo/sample image** button — let people who don't have a primed mini handy see
  a result in one click. This dramatically raises conversion from "curious" to "wow".
- **The scope is stated up front and kindly**: "Works best on primed (black/grey)
  minis shot with a side light." Manage expectations so painted-mini uploads don't
  read as "it's broken."
- **Mobile-viewable results**, even if editing is desktop-first — most people click
  your link from a phone.
- Free-tier Render **cold-start**: first load can be slow. A tiny "waking up…" state
  beats a blank screen when a crowd hits it at once.

## 7. Monetization (later, optional — keep in back pocket)

Only relevant after real usage. Non-mutually-exclusive paths, least to most involved:
- **Tip jar / Ko-fi / "buy me a paint"** link — zero friction, community-friendly.
- **Affiliate paint links** — recommend a paint, link to buy it. Natural given the
  per-layer recommendations; a possible company conversation too.
- **Pro tier** — save projects, PDF export, more regions, coloured-mini support (your
  roadmap items *are* the premium features).
- **Brand sponsorship / white-label** — a paint company funds it as a marketing tool
  for their range. This is the "get money out of it" endgame, and it's realistic only
  once you can show usage.

---

## TL;DR

1. Build the **before/after slider + a 15s GIF**. Nothing happens without them.
2. Lead every pitch with the **"on your own photo, layer by layer"** hook.
3. Roll out **friends → Reddit/Discord → creators → companies**, gathering proof at
   each stage.
4. Kill friction (**no login, demo image, cold-start state**) *before* going public.
5. Approach companies as a **paint-recommendation partner**, not a salesperson —
   money is the last stage, not the first.
